Student List Wins Outright Majority in Serbian Parliamentary Polls

Katarina Baletic
Belgrade
BIRN
October 26, 202612:07
Latest figures confirm that the Student List won an outright majority in the parliamentary elections, ending the 14-year rule of the Serbian Progressive Party and strongman leader Aleksandar Vucic.

The latest results issued by the Republic Electoral Commission (RIK) on Monday show that the opposition Student List won an outright majority in Serbia’s snap parliamentary elections with 52.65 per cent of the vote, while the Serbian Progressive Party’s “United Serbia” electoral list, led by Aleksandar Vucic, won 39.2 per cent.

The results are based on vote counts from 8,135 polling stations out of a total of 8,392. Voter turnout was 68.8 per cent, according to RIK.

The results published by non-governmental election monitors vary only slightly.

The Centre for Free Elections and Democracy (CeSID) and Ipsos, which jointly monitored the elections, announced on Monday that, according to 96 per cent of their representative sample, the Student List received 52.9 per cent of the vote, while United Serbia won 38.8 per cent.

According to their projections, the Student List is expected to win 140 seats in the legislature, while United Serbia is projected to secure 103 seats. This means that the Student List could form a government independently without having to seek a coalition.

The remaining seats are expected to go to electoral lists representing national minorities. The Alliance of Vojvodina Hungarians is projected to win three seats and the Albanian Coalition is expected to win two seats.

The “Rasim Ljajic – The People (SDP–CDP)” list and “SDA Sandzak – Guardians of Sandzak” are each projected to win one seat. Both represent the Bosniak national minority.

The Student List was formed by the student movement, which led almost two years of protests following the Novi Sad railway station canopy collapse in November 2024 that killed 16 people.

Its candidate list was mostly made up of experts and professors chosen by students. They still haven’t presented their prime ministerial candidate, although the first name on the candidate list was Ilija Srdanovic, a professor at the Medical Faculty of Novi Sad University.
